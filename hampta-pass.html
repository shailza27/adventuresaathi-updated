Hampta Pass Crossing

Few treks in India change the landscape as dramatically as Hampta Pass — you walk out of the pine forests and green meadows of Kullu and drop, in a single pass crossing, into the stark brown desert of Lahaul. The contrast is the whole point.

Overview

What this trek is actually like

Starting from Jobra near Manali, the trail follows the Rani Nallah river through birch and maple forest before the valley opens up around Balu Ka Ghera, a wide grazing meadow ringed by peaks. The pass crossing itself on day three is the technical heart of the trek — a steep scramble up loose scree, rewarded with the sudden, jarring shift into Lahaul's high-desert terrain on the far side.

Most batches add on an optional side trip to Chandratal, the moon lake, before driving back to Manali — it's a natural extension since you're already on the Lahaul side.

Day by day

The itinerary

A typical batch schedule — exact timings shift a little with weather and group pace.

Day1

Manali to Jobra to Chika

Drive to the roadhead at Jobra, then a short trek to the first campsite at Chika through pine forest.

~2 hrs drive + 3 km trek
Day2

Chika to Balu Ka Ghera

A longer day through birch forest opening into wide grazing meadows.

7 km5–6 hrs trek
Day3

Balu Ka Ghera to Shea Goru via Hampta Pass

The main event — a steep climb to the pass at 14,100 ft, then descend into Lahaul terrain.

8 km7–8 hrs trek
Day4

Shea Goru to Chatru (optional Chandratal)

Descend to the roadhead at Chatru; many batches detour to Chandratal lake here.

7 km trek + optional Chandratal drive
Day5

Chatru to Manali

Scenic drive back over Rohtang, returning to Manali by evening.

~4–5 hrs drive

Before you go

Best time & what to pack

Best time to go

  • Mid-June to September — the pass is snow-free and river crossings are manageable
  • July–August is peak monsoon in Kullu, so trails can be wetter and slippery on the lower stretches
  • September offers the clearest skies and driest trail conditions

What to pack

  • Sturdy waterproof trekking shoes (river crossings are frequent)
  • A change of dry socks and shoes for camp
  • Trekking poles — essential for the scree descent off the pass
  • Warm layers for Balu Ka Ghera and Shea Goru nights (can drop below freezing)
  • Sun protection — high-altitude sun on the Lahaul side is intense
